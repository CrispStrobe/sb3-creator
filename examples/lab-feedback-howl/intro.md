# Feedback howl: an earphone held over the speaker

An amplifier listens through an earphone and plays through a loudspeaker. Hold the earphone close to the speaker and the circuit starts to howl on its own: the same ear-splitting whistle a stage microphone makes in front of its loudspeaker.

**Teaches:** positive feedback, loop gain, sound falling off with distance

## What to do

Click **Sim**. In the instruments column, the **Distance** slider says how far the earphone is from the speaker. Start at 3 cm, then move it to 20 cm and back. Turn the volume pot down to half and try again.

## What you should see

- Close and at full volume: a loud steady howl, a few hundred hertz.
- Far away: a short thump at switch-on, then silence.
- Volume at half: silence, even close up.

## Why it works

Whatever the speaker plays reaches the earphone, gets amplified two hundred times and is played again. If each round trip comes back stronger than it left, the tiniest disturbance grows until the amplifier runs out of voltage: that is the howl. Distance and volume both set how much comes back. Moving the microphone away or turning down the volume is exactly how a sound engineer stops it.
