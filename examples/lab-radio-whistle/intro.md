# Two stations too close: the whistle, and how to tune more sharply

Broadcast stations keep a fixed distance on the dial for a reason. This crystal set hears a strong local station and, only 9 kHz away, a neighbour. Listen for the high whistle that the two make together, then move the antenna from the coil's tap to its top and hear what that does to the tuning.

**Teaches:** beat frequency, channel spacing, selectivity, matching the antenna with a tap

## What to do

Click **Sim** and listen to the earphone. Then move the slide switch to the right and turn the tuning knob down to about 0.1 to find the local station again.

## What you should see

- A 400 Hz tone with a faint high whistle above it: 9 kHz, the distance between the two carriers.
- With the antenna on the top of the coil: louder, but now a second tone, 1 kHz from a station 90 kHz away, mixes in as well.

## Why it works

Two carriers at 612 kHz and 621 kHz reach the diode together. Their sum gets stronger when their swings line up and weaker when they cancel, 9000 times a second, and the diode recovers that as a whistle. The coil's tap connects the antenna to only a quarter of the turns, so the antenna loads the resonant circuit lightly and it stays sharp. Connected to the top, the antenna damps it: more signal arrives, but the circuit no longer tells nearby stations apart so well.
