## What you see
Four transistors, four LEDs. Two light, two stay dark — and in each one only two of the three legs are used.

## Try this
1. Click **Sim**. Which LEDs light?
2. Follow each circuit: is the base at the plus side or at the minus side?
3. Compare A with C, and B with D. They are the same junction, just turned round.

## What is going on
Inside an NPN transistor, a thin P-type layer (the base) sits between two N-type layers. That gives two diodes that share their anode: base–emitter and base–collector. From the base outward current flows; inward it is blocked. Knowing this, you can find the base of an unknown transistor with a battery and an LED. What two loose diodes cannot do is amplify — that needs the base to be very thin, so that most electrons fly straight through to the collector.
