# A click without a wire

Press a button, and an earphone a hand's width away clicks, although no wire runs between them. A current switched on in one coil sends a pulse into a second coil through its magnetic field.

**Teaches:** induction by a changing current, only change induces, a diode as a detector, the field's fall-off with distance

## What to do

Click **Sim** and press the push button. Hold it, then let go. The sending coil's dial is the distance between the two coils: turn it and press again.

## What you should see

- Each press: a short pulse at the earphone (a few tenths of a volt), a click.
- Holding the button: nothing more. A steady current induces nothing.
- Letting go: the pulse in the second coil is reversed, and the diode blocks it.
- Coils further apart: a much weaker click.

## Why it works

Switching the current on makes the first coil's magnetic field grow within a few microseconds. That growing field passes through the second coil and induces a voltage there for as long as it changes. The germanium diode lets the positive pulse through to the crystal earphone. A coil's field weakens with the cube of the distance, so twice the distance gives an eighth of the click. This bench couples the two coils through the magnetic field close to them. Hertz's first experiments used sparks and short wires instead, which send out electric waves.
