# Hearing limit: a whistle that climbs out of earshot

Two transistors make a very high whistle, and a knob pushes it higher still, past 20 kHz. Somewhere on the way you will stop hearing it, although the circuit keeps whistling: that is the upper limit of your hearing. A slide switch adds a capacitor that moves the whole range lower.

**Teaches:** transistor astable multivibrator, voltage-controlled pitch, the upper limit of hearing

## What to do

Click **Sim** and listen at the earphone. Turn the pitch knob slowly up and notice where the tone vanishes for you. Then flip the slide switch and turn the knob again.

## What you should see

- The tone rises from about 12 kHz (knob at 0) to about 22 kHz (knob at full).
- With the extra capacitor it runs from about 5.5 to 11.6 kHz.

## Why it works

The transistors switch on in turn. After each switch, one base capacitor is pulled back up through a resistor until its transistor turns on again. The knob sets the voltage those resistors pull towards: a higher voltage, a faster pull, a higher tone. A bigger capacitor takes longer to pull, so the tone is lower.
