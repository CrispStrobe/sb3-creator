# A charged comb: a bending water jet and sparks

Run a comb through dry hair and hold it next to a thin stream of water: the stream bends towards it. Keep combing and the comb can even throw a small spark. Here a light barrier sees the stream move, and an LED flashes at every spark.

**Teaches:** electric charge and induction, kilovolts on a small capacitance, breakdown of air

## What to do

Click **Sim**. Turn the light path's knob (the comb's distance from the stream) from far to close. Then slide **COMB** to combing and watch the SPARK LED; slide it back to rest.

## What you should see

- Comb far: BEAM dark. Comb close: the stream bends out of the light and BEAM lights.
- Combing: about two sparks a second, each a flash of the yellow LED. Resting: none.

## Why it works

The charged comb draws charge of the opposite sign onto the earthed water stream and then pulls on it, whichever sign the comb has. The stream moves out of the light beam and the phototransistor gets more light. Combing loads a tiny capacitance, the comb, with a tiny current; its voltage climbs to several thousand volts, until the air in a one-millimetre gap breaks down. The spark's charge, caught on a capacitor, lights the LED for a moment.
