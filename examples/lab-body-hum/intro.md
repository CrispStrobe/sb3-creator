# Your body hums

Touch the input of a sensitive amplifier with your finger and the earphone buzzes at 50 Hz. The signal comes from you: your body picks up the mains wiring around you like an antenna.

**Teaches:** the body as an antenna, capacitive coupling, AC amplifier

## What to do

Click **Sim**. Turn the touch plate's knob from 0 (not touching) to 1 (firm touch).

## What you should see

- Not touching: silence.
- Touching: a steady 50 Hz hum in the earphone; at the input there are only a few millivolts.

## Why it works

You stand in a room full of mains wiring. Your body is a conductor, coupled by a few picofarads to the wires and by about 150 pF to the floor, so it sits at a few volts of 50 Hz. When you touch the plate, a tiny current flows from you through your skin into the amplifier. Two transistor stages multiply it until the earphone hums. This is why microphone inputs are screened.
