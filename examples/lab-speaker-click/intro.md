# A loudspeaker on a battery: it only clicks

Connect a loudspeaker straight to a battery and you might expect a hum or a tone. You get one click, then silence. Let go and there is a second click. The cone moves when the current changes, not while it flows.

**Teaches:** a loudspeaker follows changes of current, current direction sets the push

## What to do

Click **Sim**. Press and hold the push button, then let go. Watch the ammeter. Now move both slide switches to the other side (together they reverse the speaker's leads) and press the button again.

## What you should see

- Press: one click; the ammeter shows about 150 mA. While you hold the button the speaker stays silent.
- Release: a second click; the current is gone.
- With both slide switches moved: the same current, but negative. The cone is now pulled in instead of pushed out.

## Why it works

The current in the voice coil pushes on the magnet. A steady current gives a steady push: the cone moves out once and then just stays there, so there is nothing to hear. Sound needs the cone to move back and forth, which happens only when the current changes: at the press and at the release. Reverse the current and the push reverses, so the cone jumps the other way.
