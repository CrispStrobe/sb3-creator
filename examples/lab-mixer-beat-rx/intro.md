# Whistling stations: a mixer with its own oscillator

The mixer chip has a transistor of its own for an oscillator. Built into a tuned circuit it runs at whatever frequency the tuning knob gives it. A wire antenna brings the stations straight into the mixer, and wherever the oscillator lands close to a station, their difference comes out of the mixer as a whistle.

**Teaches:** local oscillator, heterodyne (beat) reception, zero beat

## What to do

Click **Sim** and listen at the start (knob at 0.45). Then turn the tuning knob slowly towards 0.53 and on to 0.535.

## What you should see

- At 0.45: nothing — no station near the oscillator.
- At 0.53: a whistle of about 1 kHz.
- At 0.535: a higher whistle, about 3 kHz. Somewhere between, the pitch drops to a growl and to nothing (zero beat).

## Why it works

The mixer multiplies the antenna's signal by its oscillator. Multiplying two frequencies gives their sum and their difference. The sum, over a megahertz, the earphone cannot follow; the difference of a few kilohertz it plays. Move the oscillator and the difference changes, so the pitch slides with the knob.
