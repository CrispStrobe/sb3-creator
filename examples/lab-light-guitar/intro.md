# A guitar string played by light

A string sits half in a light beam. Pluck it and it swings in and out of the beam, so the light flickers at the string's note. A phototransistor and an earphone make the flicker audible: an electric guitar without a magnet.

**Teaches:** an optical pickup, modulating light by motion

## What to do

Click **Sim** and listen. Turn the knob to tune the string.

## What you should see

- A 110 Hz note that dies away and is plucked again every second.
- Tuned up, up to 330 Hz.

## Why it works

The string shades more or less of the beam as it swings, so the light on the phototransistor rises and falls at the string's frequency. The phototransistor's current follows, and the capacitor passes only that changing part to the earphone.
