# A red LED sees green light, a green LED does not see red

Turn an LED round and it becomes a tiny light receiver: light falling on it makes a current of a fraction of a microampere. An op-amp turns that current into volts. Try which colours each LED can see.

**Teaches:** photon energy, LEDs as light receivers, the transimpedance amplifier

## What to do

Click **Sim**. The red lamp is on and the red receiver is selected. Use the slide switch to change to the green receiver, then switch the red lamp off and the green lamp on, and try both receivers again.

## What you should see

- Red lamp: the red receiver gives about 0.45 V, the green receiver nothing at all.
- Green lamp: the red receiver gives about 0.9 V, the green one only 0.16 V.

## Why it works

Light comes in packets, photons, and the shorter the wavelength the more energy each one carries. Inside an LED a photon can only free an electron if it brings at least as much energy as the LED itself sends out with its own light. A green photon carries more than a red LED needs, so the red LED sees green light. A red photon is too weak for the green LED: however bright the red lamp, the green LED stays blind to it.
