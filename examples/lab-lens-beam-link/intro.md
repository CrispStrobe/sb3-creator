# A lens turns an LED into a beam that reaches 3 m

An LED's light spreads in all directions and is gone after a metre. Put a lens in front of it and the light leaves as a beam, enough to carry a tone across a room to a phototransistor and a two-transistor amplifier.

**Teaches:** lenses, the inverse-square law, optical communication

## What to do

Click **Sim** and listen to the earphone. Move the knob of the light path (the distance) between 50 cm and 5 m.

## What you should see

- At 3 m the tone is still loud.
- The further away, the weaker the signal at the phototransistor.

## Why it works

Without a lens the light spreads into a cone, and at twice the distance the same light covers four times the area. The lens catches most of the cone and turns it into an almost parallel beam, which spreads only a little. The receiver uses capacitors between its stages, so it ignores the steady room light and amplifies only the flashing, about 1000 times a second, which the earphone plays as a tone.
