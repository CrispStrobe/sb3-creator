# A scale from a ruler, a magnet and a Hall sensor

Hang weights on the end of a ruler: it bends, and a magnet on its tip comes closer to a Hall sensor. An op-amp turns the change into a meter reading. A working scale, with one surprise in its dial.

**Teaches:** Hooke's law, magnetic fields, calibrating a sensor

## What to do

Click **Sim**. In the instruments column use the **Load** slider or its buttons (0, 50, 100, 200 g) and watch the meter. Hover over the ruler to see how far it has bent and how strong the field is.

## What you should see

- 0 g: the meter rests at about 0.
- 50 g: about 0.9 V.
- 100 g: about 4.2 V, almost five times as much, not twice.
- 200 g: the needle is at the end of the scale.

## Why it works

The ruler bends exactly in proportion to the weight. But the magnet's field grows much faster than the distance shrinks: half the distance gives eight times the field. So the needle moves little for the first grams and a lot for the last. To use this as a scale you hang known weights on it and mark each one on the dial. Real kitchen scales use a strain gauge instead, which is linear.
