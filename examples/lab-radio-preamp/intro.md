# One more transistor makes the radio loud

A detector receiver with a short antenna drives a loudspeaker amplifier. The station is weak, so the diode hands over only a few millivolts of sound. A slide switch puts a one-transistor audio stage in between — or leaves it out.

**Teaches:** gain of a single transistor stage, gains multiply

## What to do

Click **Sim** and listen (switch in the second position: with the transistor). Then flip the **slide switch** to the first position, and back.

## What you should see

- With the transistor stage the 400 Hz tone is loud (about 1.6 V at the amplifier's output).
- Without it the tone almost disappears (about 0.02 V).

## Why it works

The power amplifier multiplies its input by 20. The transistor stage, with its bias set by the resistor from collector to base, multiplies by several tens on its own. Chained, the gains multiply: the same few millivolts from the detector become a loud tone.
