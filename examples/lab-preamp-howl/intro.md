# Earphone, preamp, amplifier: the feedback whistle

An earphone works as a microphone, too. Feed it through a one-transistor preamp and the amplifier chip into a loudspeaker, then bring it near that loudspeaker: the circuit starts to whistle by itself. How close you can get depends on the volume.

**Teaches:** a preamp stage ahead of the amplifier, positive feedback, loop gain

## What to do

Click **Sim**. In the instruments column the **Distance** slider sets how far the earphone is from the loudspeaker; it starts at 5 cm. Wait a moment, then move it to 40 cm and back. Turn the volume pot down to a tenth and bring the earphone close again.

## What you should see

- At switch-on: a short thump while the preamp settles.
- Close and at full volume: a loud, steady whistle of a few kilohertz.
- Far away, or with the volume turned down: silence.

## Why it works

What the loudspeaker plays reaches the earphone, which turns it back into a voltage. The transistor amplifies it about thirty-five times, the amplifier chip twenty times more, and the loudspeaker plays it again. If each round trip comes back stronger than it went out, the smallest disturbance grows until the amplifier runs out of voltage: that is the whistle. Distance and the volume pot decide how much comes back.
