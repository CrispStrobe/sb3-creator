# Audit ledger — pc13 … pc24

Layer-2 slice per `reference/example-quality.md`. One entry per example: the
checklist, the numbers it was measured with, the verdict, and the action.

**Coverage for this whole slice: `layers: engine`.** Every verdict below was
produced by driving the engine — `node scripts/audit-solve.mjs <id>` on its
flat path, and on its fabric path (`Circuit.fromJSON`, the app's real loader:
hole occupancy → strip nets → mergeNets → setNetlist) for the three breadboard
examples. No browser was driven; no claim below is about rendering, canvas
ghosts or console errors.

**Observation conditions** are recorded per entry, because for the two RC
examples the timebase decides the outcome outright.

## pc13-direct-diode — VERDICT: content-fix

**Loads and solves.** 4 parts → 4 nets, no unknown kinds or terminals, no DRC
warnings. Category `pure-circuit`, difficulty 1, title "Diode, direct wired" —
all three match what solves. Instruments: none needed; a pure circuit with no
debugger attached. ✓

**Phenomenon observable.** ✓ The titled thing — a diode in series eating part
of the loop's voltage — is directly readable: 0.796 V across the diode, and the
LED lights dimmer than it would without it.

**Finding — EXPECTED.md's current was wrong by 10 %.** It claimed
`I = (5.0 − 0.7 − 2.0) / 220 ≈ 10.5 mA`. Measured at t = 1 ms:

| node | measured |
|---|---|
| R top | 5.0000 V |
| R bottom = diode anode | 2.8917 V |
| diode cathode = LED anode | 2.0958 V |

→ I = (5.0000 − 2.8917) / 220 = **9.58 mA**, not 10.5 mA. The brightness figure
(0.4792) was right, which is how the wrong current survived: only one of the
two numbers was ever checked.

The cause is not an engine defect. Both junctions sit *above* their nominal
drop at this current — diode 0.796 V (quoted 0.7), LED 2.096 V (quoted 2.0) —
because a diode's forward drop rises with current. The textbook subtraction is
an approximation and the 10 % gap is the physics.

**Action.** EXPECTED.md rewritten around the measured node table, with the
ideal-drop calculation kept and explicitly labelled as the approximation it is
— the discrepancy is now the lesson rather than an unnoticed error.
intro.md + intro.de.md written (beginner / 8+).

Observation conditions: DC, single sample at t = 1 ms. Nothing transient here.

## pc14-mini-led — VERDICT: content-fix (the example was electrically dead)

**Finding 1 — 0 nets. The circuit did not exist.** Through the app's own
loader:

```
[fabric path: Circuit.fromJSON — 4 parts, 0 nets, vcc 5]
── t = 1 ms
(nothing)
```

No nets, no nodes, no LED. A layer-1 liveness failure of the most complete
kind: not "all voltages zero" but "no voltage nodes at all".

**Cause — the wire endpoint dialect.** The two supply wires were written flat:

```json
{ "from": "vsource_2", "to": "breadboard_1", "toTerminal": "e3" }
```

`Circuit.fromJSON` normalises that to a **part** endpoint
`{part: "breadboard_1", terminal: "e3"}`. But `_syncNetlist` filters
`kind === 'breadboard'` out of the engine parts, and folds strip nets in by
looking for `e.board` — which a part endpoint does not have. So the tap never
reached a strip, the seated resistor and LED stayed islands, and the netlist
came out empty. A breadboard hole must be addressed as
`{"board": "breadboard_1", "hole": "e3"}`; both dialects are legal in
`circuit-json-roundtrip.test.mjs`, and only one works.

**Action — both wires rewritten to the `{board, hole}` dialect.** No part,
value or hole was moved: the seating was already correct.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| nets | 0 | 3 |
| LED anode | — | 2.0625 V |
| LED current | — | 6.250 mA |
| LED brightness | — | 0.3125 |

The brightness now matches EXPECTED.md's shipped 0.3125 exactly — the file had
been describing a circuit that never ran. Its current figure (6.4 mA) was the
ideal-drop estimate and is 2 % high; corrected to the measured 6.25 mA.

EXPECTED.md rewritten with the seating table and the strip reasoning;
intro.md + intro.de.md written (beginner / 8+).

Observation conditions: DC, t = 1 ms.

## pc15-mini-npn — VERDICT: engine-bug (a content-fix was applied first)

**Finding 1 (content) — the transistor was not in the circuit.** Same 0-net
death as pc14, plus a seating that made no sense once the nets were restored:

- the 10 kΩ "base" resistor was seated a2 → d6, i.e. **in parallel with the
  470 Ω load**, not feeding the base at all;
- the NPN sat on columns 8/9/10, connected to nothing — the LED's cathode was
  on column 7;
- the return lead tapped e10, i.e. the bare collector.

So even with the wire dialect fixed, this was a resistor, an LED and an
unconnected transistor.

**Action (content).** Re-seated to a real low-side switch — base resistor
col-t2 → col-t8, NPN emitter/base/collector on adjacent columns 7/8/9 (as a
TO-92 actually sits), 470 Ω + LED from col-t2 down to the collector at col-t9,
supply taps at e2 and e7. Full table in EXPECTED.md.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| nets | 0 | 5 |
| base voltage | — | 0.7043 V |
| base current | — | 0.43 mA |

The base half is now exactly as designed: V_be = 0.7043 V, I_b = 0.43 mA,
matching EXPECTED.md's shipped "Ib ≈ 0.43 mA".

**Finding 2 (ENGINE-BUG) — the collector half is unphysical.** With the
circuit correct, `audit-solve pc15-mini-npn`:

```
   -15.1898 V  led_4.anode,resistor_3.b
     0.7043 V  npn_5.base,resistor_6.b
   -17.6194 V  led_4.cathode,npn_5.collector
  LED led_4: brightness 1.0000, I = 42.957 mA
  DRC: led_4: current 43.0 mA exceeds 20 mA rating.
```

42.957 mA is **β · I_b exactly** (100 × 0.43 mA). The collector branch cannot
supply it: 5 V − 2 V (LED) − 0.2 V (V_ce_sat) over 470 Ω is at most 5.96 mA.
A real transistor saturates; this one drives the collector to −17.6 V to
deliver a current the loop physically cannot carry. Escalation **E1** below.

EXPECTED.md states the designed numbers and, separately, the measured ones with
the defect named. intro.md + intro.de.md written (intermediate / 12+) carrying
a dated known-defect note so no learner is told to expect a reading that will
not appear.

Observation conditions: DC, t = 1 ms; also probed at 0.001 ms — the negative
excursion is the DC operating point, not a transient.

## pc19-buzzer-direct — VERDICT: engine-bug

**Loads and solves.** 3 parts → 3 nets, no warnings, category and difficulty 1
sensible. The electrical half is exactly right: buzzer + at **2.5000 V**, a
clean half-divider, because the engine stamps the buzzer as a fixed 100 Ω
(`mna.js: stampBuzzerResistance`) against the 100 Ω series resistor. I = 25 mA.

**Finding — the titled phenomenon cannot happen.** `buzzerTone()` returns
`{hz: 0, on: false}`, and no change to this circuit can alter that. Tones come
only from `_recordBuzzerEdges(pin)` (`board.js:2498`), which appends an edge
only when a part of kind **`mcu`** shares a net with the buzzer. A circuit with
no MCU has an empty edge list, and `buzzerTone` returns off on `edges.length < 2`.

This is not merely a missing feature: bw-board's own source draws the
active/passive distinction and puts this part on the active side —
`devices/digital-ics.js:213`, introducing the separate `piezo` model, reads
*"Unlike a buzzer (which has a built-in oscillator), a piezo needs an external
signal."* An active buzzer on DC is exactly what pc19 builds, and it is exactly
what the model claims `buzzer` is. Intent and implementation disagree.
Escalation **E2**.

**Action.** Circuit deliberately **not** modified — it is the correct circuit
for the part it names, and rewiring it would hide the defect. EXPECTED.md now
separates what is measurable (the divider, 25 mA) from what is blocked (the
sound), with the mechanism named. intro.md + intro.de.md written (beginner /
8+) carrying a dated known-defect note; their teaching content is the
active-vs-passive distinction, which is what the example is actually for.

Observation conditions: DC, t = 1 ms; `buzzerTone` also read at 100 ms and
1000 ms in case of staleness logic — off at every sample.

## pc24-light-gate — VERDICT: engine-bug (a content-fix was applied first)

**Finding 1 (content) — the LDR's declared range was inert.** `circuit.json`
declared `minOhms: 1000, maxOhms: 100000`. The device reads **`rDark` /
`rLight`** (`mna.js: stampVariableResistor`; `board.js:2151`), so both were

found with `R` vs `windingR`: an unknown param name is accepted without a
warning.

Consequence, measured before the fix: at the reset (dark) control the base sat
at **0.0495 V**, which is 5 · 10k/(1 MΩ + 10k) — the 1 MΩ default, not the
declared 100 kΩ. At full brightness the 100 Ω leg drove the solver to
**−3894 V** with *"Circuit did not converge"*.

**Action (content).** Renamed to `rDark: 100000, rLight: 1000`.

| light | base before | base after |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (dark) | 0.0495 V | **0.4545 V** |
| 1 (bright) | 1.0899 V | 0.7418 V |

The sensing half is now correct and is the working half of the example: the
base tracks the light across 0.4545 → 0.7418 V and crosses V_be where the
divider says it should.

**Finding 2 (content) — the stated direction was backwards.** `program.bw`

divider leg, so bright light pulls the base up: it turns on in **light**.

**Finding 3 (ENGINE-BUG) — same defect as pc15 and pc23.**

| light | base | collector | LED current |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.4545 V | 3.0000 V | 0.000 mA |
| 0.5 | 0.7036 V | **−33.2874 V** | 35.9 mA |
| 1.0 | 0.7418 V | **−419.5816 V** | 418.4 mA (+ non-convergence DRC) |

EXPECTED.md rewritten; intro.md + intro.de.md written (advanced / 12+) with a

# Escalations

## E1 — ~~ENGINE-BUG~~ RESOLVED, bw-board `c378bb0` (2026-08-15): NPN/PNP saturation

`stampNPN` (`bw-board/src/mna.js:1278–1319`) stamps the collector current as an
unconditional voltage-controlled current source:

```js
const gm  = beta * gEq;   // Ic = β · Ib
const ic0 = beta * iEq;
```

There is no V_ce_sat floor, no forward-active/saturation region test, and no
clamp anywhere downstream — `grep -i 'vce\|satur' src/mna.js src/board.js`
returns only op-amp rail handling and MOSFET comments. `stampPNP` is the same
code mirrored and will have the same defect.

**Consequence.** Whenever a circuit asks for more collector current than the
load branch can supply — which is the *defining condition of a transistor used
as a switch* — the solver satisfies I_c = β · I_b by driving the collector node
arbitrarily negative:

| example | I_b | β · I_b demanded | branch can pass | collector |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pc23 (button closed) | 0.43 mA | 43 mA | ~6 mA | −17.62 V |
| pc15 (10 kΩ base) | 0.43 mA | 43 mA | ~6 mA | −17.62 V |
| pc24 (light 0.5) | 0.36 mA | 36 mA | ~2.8 mA | −33.29 V |
| pc24 (light 1.0) | 4.18 mA | 418 mA | ~2.8 mA | −419.58 V + non-convergence |

Minimal repro, no breadboard, no sensor:

```
node scripts/audit-solve.mjs pc23-transistor-switch --set button1=1
```

**Why it was not caught earlier.** Gallery NPN examples that pass do so by
accident of component values: `pc05-npn-switch` uses a 100 kΩ base resistor, so
β · I_b = 4.3 mA stays *below* what the load can pass and the transistor never
leaves the forward-active region. The engine is therefore fine for BJT
*amplifiers* and wrong for BJT *switches* — and the gallery is mostly switches.

**Suggested fix.** Add a saturation region to `stampNPN`/`stampPNP` in the same
shape as the op-amp's existing region handling (`mna.js:1526–1566`): once
V_ce falls below V_ce_sat (~0.2 V), stop stamping the β · I_b source and stamp
a low resistance (~1–20 Ω) collector-to-emitter instead, iterating the region
choice in the Newton loop alongside `opampRegions`.

**Blast radius.** Every example with an `npn` or `pnp` driving a load into
saturation. Within this slice: pc15, pc23, pc24. Outside it, at minimum
`38-npn-switch`, `pc39-nmos-switch` (check — the MOSFET stamp is separate),
`44-darlington-motor` and `pc62-motor-indicator` deserve a re-solve once the
clamp lands.

**RESOLVED:** bw-board `c378bb0` (2026-08-15) adds `bjtRegions` map alongside
`opampRegions`. When Vce < Vce_sat (~0.2 V), the VCCS is replaced with a stiff
conductance clamp holding Ic to what the load passes. PNP gets mirrored clamp.
Regression test `bjt-regions.test.mjs`: NPN saturated Vce ≈ 0.2 V, PNP
conducting collector > 2.0 V. All gallery NPN/PNP examples now produce correct
voltages.

## E2 — ~~ENGINE-BUG~~ RESOLVED, bw-board `39b0c10` (2026-08-15): buzzer DC

`buzzerTone()` (`board.js:747`) derives frequency purely from `buzzerEdges`,
which is appended to only by `_recordBuzzerEdges(pin)` (`board.js:2498`) — and
that function requires a part of kind **`mcu`** on the buzzer's net. There is no
other producer of buzzer edges anywhere in `src/`.

So any buzzer not driven by an MCU pin reports `{hz: 0, on: false}` forever,
regardless of the voltage across it. `pc19-buzzer-direct` measures a perfectly
correct 2.5 V and 25 mA through the part and is silent.

This contradicts the engine's own model of what a `buzzer` *is*.
`devices/digital-ics.js:213`, introducing `piezo`, reads:

> *"Unlike a buzzer (which has a built-in oscillator), a piezo needs an
> external signal."*

An active buzzer with a built-in oscillator sounds on DC — that is the entire
difference between the two parts, and the `buzzer` kind is the one that is
supposed to have it. Repro: `node scripts/audit-solve.mjs pc19-buzzer-direct`,
then read `buzzerTone('buzzer_3')`.

**Suggested fix.** Give `buzzer` a self-oscillating mode: when the voltage
across it exceeds a threshold and no MCU edges are being recorded, synthesise
edges at a `params.hz` (default ~2 kHz, the typical active-buzzer note) so
`buzzerTone` reports on. Passive elements already have their own kind
(`piezo`), so this does not blur the distinction — it restores it.

**Blast radius.** Every MCU-less buzzer example. Within this slice: pc19.
Outside it: `pc53-buzzer-switch` at least.

**RESOLVED:** bw-board `39b0c10` (2026-08-15). `buzzerTone()` falls back to
rated tone (`params.toneHz`, default 2400 Hz) when powered DC >2 V. Power-off
silences it. `pc19-buzzer-direct` now reports `{hz: 2400, on: true}`.

## E3 — ~~GALLERY TRAP~~ RESOLVED, bw-board `39b0c10` (2026-08-15): `rgb_led` composite

`rgb_led` appears in `BoardImpl.getPartKinds()`, in `types.js`, in
`current-ratings.js` and in `bw-circuit-ui`'s `terminalsForKind` — so it
validates, renders and looks entirely usable. It has no electrical model:
`validate.js:51` is `rgb_led: null, // composite`, `mna.js:578` lists it under
*"handled elsewhere or composite"* and stamps nothing, and nothing expands it
into the `<id>_r` / `<id>_g` / `<id>_b` sub-LEDs that `rgbLedBrightness()`
(`board.js:792`) reads.

Measured — 5 V → 330 Ω → `rgb_led.r_anode`, cathode → gnd:

```
     5.0000 V  src.pos,r1.a
     5.0000 V  r1.b,rgb.r_anode      ← no drop
     0.0000 V  rgb.cathode,gnd.gnd
```

No drop, no current, no brightness. Any example built on it would pass every
existing gate and teach nothing. This is what stopped `pc20-rgb-mix` from being
rebuilt around the part its title named.

**Suggested fix.** Either implement the composite expansion (three `led`
sub-parts sharing the cathode, which `rgbLedBrightness` already expects), or
remove `rgb_led` from the advertised kind list until it exists. The current
state — advertised, validating, inert — is the worst of the three.

**RESOLVED:** bw-board `39b0c10` (2026-08-15). `setNetlist()` now expands
composites: `rgb_led` → `<id>_r`/`<id>_g`/`<id>_b` sub-LEDs with real diode
drops. `seven_segment` similarly expanded. `KNOWN_INERT` is now EMPTY.

**Layer-1 hardening candidate (from E3 and from pc24's `minOhms`).** Two
distinct silent-acceptance classes found in this slice:

1. **A part kind with no stamp and no device model.** Mechanically checkable:
   for every kind in `getPartKinds()`, assert it is either handled in `mna.js`'s
   switch or registered in `devices.js`. `rgb_led` would fail today.
2. **An unknown `params` key.** `windingR` (33-inductive), `minOhms`/`maxOhms`
   (pc24) and `R` were all accepted and discarded without a warning. A
   per-kind allow-list of parameter names, warning on anything outside it,
   would have caught all three at authoring time.
