# Audit ledger — pc25 … pc36

Layer-2 slice, doctrine `reference/example-quality.md` (Layers 2–3 + the
2026-08-15 amendments). One entry per example: verdict, coverage, the
observation conditions the numbers were taken under, and the fix as measured.

**Coverage for this whole slice: `layers: engine`.** Every number below comes
from `node scripts/audit-solve.mjs` driving the real bw-board engine through the
app's loader. The browser was never opened, so the two render-side checklist
items — *loads without console errors / canvas shows every part (no ghosts)* and
*instruments shown are the relevant ones* — are **unchecked** for all twelve.
One fix was deliberately narrowed because of that limit (see pc33).

**Observation conditions.** Steady-state examples sampled at `--at 1`. Anything
switched or timed was driven with the harness's `--set` / `--set@ms` / `--power`
controls, and the sample times are quoted per entry. The two inductive examples
(pc26, and pc25's coil) were checked at 0.1 µs … 100 µs per the flyback lesson
in `AUDIT.md`; for pc26 the sample step *is* the finding.

**Harness note for later auditors.** `--set` is applied *after* `setNetlist`,
which has already solved once at t=0 and recorded an LED brightness sample. LED
`brightness` is a windowed integration, so it lags the current by tens of ms and
can show a stale pre-`--set` value (pc34 reversed reads `brightness 0.3088` at
`I = 0.000 mA`). **Read the current, not the brightness**, when using `--set`.

## pc25-relay-isolator — VERDICT: pass

`layers: engine`. Sampled with `--set sw1=1 --at 1,4,6,10`.

Pure-circuit, comment-only `program.bw`, no MCU part — layer-1 rule 1 needs no
`mcuIsProp`. Category `pure-circuit`, difficulty 3, title "Relay isolator": all
three match what the circuit does.

The titled phenomenon is observable and the numbers are right. Coil open: the
`no` contact sits at 2.00 V with 0.00 mA in the load branch (an open contact,
not a broken example — the 9 V supply node keeps layer-1 liveness satisfied).
Coil closed: 5.00 V across a 200 Ω coil = 25 mA, and the contact closes **between
the 4 ms and 6 ms samples**, which is `switchTimeMs: 5` behaving exactly as
declared. Load branch then carries 6.93 mA from the 9 V supply.

Both declared params are live: `coilR` and `switchTimeMs` are read at
`bw-board/src/devices/relay.js:29` and `:63`. The isolation the title claims is
structurally real — two supplies, no shared net but ground.

**No content change.** EXPECTED.md gained the measured table (it previously
described the behaviour without a single number); the circuit is untouched.

## pc26-motor-clamp — VERDICT: content-fix (engine defect already on file)

`layers: engine`. Sampled with `--set sw1=1 --set@10 sw1=0`, turn-off at
t = 10 ms, and the sample step swept 100 µs → 0.1 µs.

**Finding — the titled phenomenon did not exist.** This is the flyback class the
pilot found broken in 33-inductive-no-flyback, and pc26 had the identical
defect: `dc_motor` models winding resistance and back-EMF only, with no
inductance anywhere in `bw-board/src`, so there was no field to collapse. As
shipped, opening the switch produced **−0.0008 V** at the switched node, at 1 µs
resolution. `d1` was decorative: the circuit was electrically identical with and
without it, and EXPECTED.md's promise ("reducing the voltage spike at the
switch") was false.

**Action.** The pilot's precedent for 33 is followed exactly: an explicit series
`inductor` `l1` (1 mH — a realistic small-motor winding, and a part
`bw-circuit-ui` already renders) between `sw1.b` and `motor1.a`, with `d1`'s
cathode moved to `l1.a` so the freewheel loop encloses the inductance. Before,
`d1` clamped a node with nothing to clamp; now it clamps the switched node.

Measured after the fix, at the switched node:

| sample step | no diode | with `d1` |
|---|---|---|
| 100 µs | −9.0 V | −3.5 V |
| 10 µs | −90.0 V | −8.1 V |
| 1 µs | −900.0 V | −9.5 V |
| 0.1 µs | −9000.0 V | −9.7 V |

Tenfold per tenfold without the diode — **unbounded**, the correct answer for an
ideal switch opening an inductive current — and **convergent** at about −9.7 V
with it. EXPECTED.md therefore teaches bounded-versus-unbounded and declines to
quote a single peak, as 33's does.

One honest caveat recorded in EXPECTED.md: the bound is
`Vf + I·rd = 0.7 V + 0.9 A × 10 Ω`, because the engine's piecewise-linear diode
has a 10 Ω dynamic resistance. The bound is model-specific; the boundedness is
the lesson. Raising `windingR` to shrink the artifact was considered and
rejected — 33 reports the same 7–10 V band, and matching it keeps the pair
comparable.

**Engine defects touched, both already in `AUDIT.md`, neither worked around:**
`dc_motor` has no inductance (this fix adds the missing physics as a part rather
than hiding the gap), and the `henries`/`henrys` split-brain is still live —
`validate.js:70`, `builder.js:83`, `board.js:992` say `henrys`; `mna.js:450` and
`:881` read `henries` and default to 1 mH. `l1` is declared `henrys: 0.001`, so
declared and swallowed values coincide and today's numbers are correct, but the
value does not scale. Both intros carry the note, and neither intro asks the
reader to change `l1` — the pilot's layer-3 hazard.

## pc27-timer-pulse — VERDICT: engine-bug + content-fix — **ESCALATED**

`layers: engine`. Sampled at 1 … 437 ms (the RC timebase; the transient here is
the whole point and needs no sub-ms sampling).

**ENGINE BUG — the 555 is dead whenever pin 5 (control) is unconnected, which is
every 555 example in the gallery.** As shipped, this astable sat at
`u1.output = 0.0000 V` forever: the capacitor never charged past 0 V, the LED
never lit, and the example demonstrated nothing.

Mechanism, `bw-board/src/devices/timer-555.js`. The model's thresholds are
derived from the control pin by reading it —
`const upperThreshold = vControl - vGnd; const lowerThreshold = upperThreshold / 2;`
(`update()`) — and the internal divider that is supposed to hold that pin at
2/3 VCC is stamped as two conductances *to the control net*
(`ctx.conductance('vcc', 'control', …)`). When the pin belongs to no net, the
divider is not formed, `read('control')` returns 0, **both thresholds collapse
to 0 V**, the lower comparator's `vTrigger < 0` can never be true, the flip-flop
never sets, and the output is stuck low with the discharge switch closed.

On real silicon the divider is internal and pin 5 is routinely left open. The
model makes an internal component conditional on external wiring.

**Blast radius — all four 555 examples in the gallery leave pin 5 unwired:**
`51-555-astable`, `pc27-timer-pulse`, `pc47-555-monostable`,
`pc58-555-audio-pulse`. The other three are in other auditors' slices; they are
reported here, not touched.

**Content fix applied (and it is not a workaround).** `c2`, 10 nF from control
to ground, is the decoupling capacitor the 555 datasheet puts on pin 5 in every
reference schematic — the circuit was incomplete without it on its own merits.
With it, the control pin sits at exactly **3.3333 V = 2/3 VCC** and the timer
runs. The engine bug is escalated regardless: a correct circuit that omits the
optional capacitor still dies silently, and three other examples do.

Measured after the fix:

| | formula | predicted | measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| output high | 0.693·(R1+R2)·C | 138.6 ms | ~139 ms (flip at 228 ms) |
| output low | 0.693·R2·C | 69.3 ms | ~68 ms (flip at 296 ms) |
| period | | 208 ms / 4.8 Hz | ~208 ms (next flip 437 ms) |

`c1` swings between 1.667 V and 3.333 V — the two thresholds — output high is
4.86 V, LED 2.83 mA. Agreement with the textbook formulas is within a sample
interval.

**Candidate layer-1 check:** for any part whose device model reads a terminal
that is unconnected in the example, warn. It would have caught this, and the
inert-param class (pc33 below, and the pilot's finding 2) is the same shape:
*a declaration the engine never sees.*

## pc28-logic-interlock — VERDICT: pass

`layers: engine`. All four input combinations solved at `--at 1` via
`--set sw_a=1,sw_b=1` and the two single-switch cases.

Truth table is complete and correct: 0.00 V out for the three non-AND cases,
4.86 V and 2.83 mA for `1,1`. The 4.86 V rather than 5.00 V is the gate's 50 Ω

Category, difficulty 3 and title all match. **No content change.** EXPECTED.md
gained the measured truth table.

## pc29-capacitor-discharge — VERDICT: content-fix

`layers: engine`. Sampled at 0.1 s … 7 s with `--set charge=1`, then
`--set@3000 charge=0 --set@3000 discharge=1`.

**Finding — EXPECTED.md described a procedure the circuit could not perform.**
It read "Open the charge path, then close the load path", and there was no load
path to close: the circuit had exactly one switch. `rload` + LED hung
permanently across the capacitor, so the "charged" capacitor was never charged —
it sat at a steady divider value with the LED trickling continuously, and the
titled event (a discharge) never happened as a distinguishable moment.

**Action.** Added the second switch the text already assumed (`discharge`, in
series with the load branch), and retuned two values so the lesson is watchable
rather than merely correct: `rcharge` 10 kΩ → 1 kΩ (charge τ 10 s → **1 s**) and
`rload` 10 kΩ → 1 kΩ (initial discharge current 0.3 mA → **2.44 mA**).

| t | capacitor | LED |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 s | 0.48 V | 0.00 mA |
| 1.0 s | 3.16 V | 0.00 mA — one τ, 63 % of 5 V |
| 2.9 s | 4.72 V | 0.00 mA — switch over here |
| 3.0 s | 4.47 V | 2.44 mA |
| 4.0 s | 2.92 V | 0.91 mA |
| 7.0 s | 2.05 V | 0.05 mA |

The discharge deliberately runs through the LED, so it flattens against the 2 V
forward voltage instead of decaying to zero — that floor is now the intro's step
5 and its closing experiment, rather than an unexplained oddity.

**Note for anyone auditing a discharge example: do not use `--power off`.** See
the engine-bug entry below; the charge does not survive it. This example
discharges through switches for that reason, and the result is trustworthy.

## pc31-bridge-rectifier — VERDICT: content-fix (the circuit was wired wrong)

`layers: engine`, `--at 1`, both polarities (`--set src=-9` for the reversed
row).

**Finding — the bridge was miswired and the LED was effectively dark.** `d3`'s
anode and `d4`'s cathode were both tied to **ground** instead of to the source's
negative terminal, and `d4`'s anode to the source instead of to ground. The
return path from the load therefore ran into a reverse-biased `d4`, so no loop
closed. The solve showed it plainly: the source floated at +2.6 V / −6.4 V
(neither terminal referenced), `r1.a` and `r1.b` both sat at 1.9 V — **zero drop
across the load resistor, hence ~0 mA** — and the example demonstrated nothing.
The all-zero liveness gate (layer-1 rule 2) does not catch this: a floating
source leaves plenty of non-zero nodes.

**Action.** Rewired to a real bridge: AC1 = `src.pos` (`d1.anode`, `d2.cathode`),
AC2 = `src.neg` (`d3.anode`, `d4.cathode`), OUT+ = `d1.cathode`+`d3.cathode` →
load, OUT− = `d2.anode`+`d4.anode` → ground. A duplicate `src.neg → d4.anode`
wire was dropped with it.

| | `src.pos` | `src.neg` | output | LED |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before, +9 V | 2.6000 V | −6.4000 V | 1.9 V | ~0 mA |
| after, +9 V | 8.2456 V | −0.7544 V | 7.4913 V | 5.437 mA |
| after, −9 V | −0.7544 V | 8.2456 V | 7.4913 V | 5.437 mA |

The two "after" rows are the example's whole claim, and they are now identical:
the source terminals swap, the output does not move. Output = 9 V − 2 × 0.7544 V,
the two-diode-drop cost of a bridge, which EXPECTED.md and both intros now name.

## pc32-pnp-high-side — VERDICT: engine-bug — **ESCALATED**, no content change

`layers: engine`, `--at 1`, with `--set sw=1` and open.

**ENGINE BUG — the PNP model never conducts.** With `sw` closed the base solves
to **0.0000 V** — meaning no base current flows at all, since any real base
current through the 10 kΩ `rb` would lift the base to about 4.3 V — and the
collector floats at 2.0005 V with **0.000 mA** in the LED branch. The circuit as
drawn is a correct high-side switch.

Mechanism, `bw-board/src/mna.js`. The Newton loop stores the PNP's junction
voltage as V_E − V_B (line ~635, positive when conducting), but `stampPNP`
negates it before use:

```js
// mna.js:1339, inside stampPNP
const vAcross = -(diodeVoltages.get(part.id) ?? 0);   // = −V_EB
const { gEq, iEq } = diodeCompanion(vAcross, vbe, rd);
```

`diodeCompanion` turns on when `vAcross >= vf`, so conduction would require the
base to sit 0.7 V *above* the emitter — the reverse of the device. The iteration
converges to permanently-off from the initial guess and stays there. `stampNPN`
(line 1292) uses the stored value unnegated and is fine, which is why every NPN
example in the gallery works.

Minimal repro, five parts, no example file needed: `vcc` → PNP emitter, base →
10 kΩ → ground, collector → 1 kΩ → ground. Expected a saturated collector near
5 V; measured base 0.0000 V, collector 0.0005 V.

**Blast radius:** `pc32-pnp-high-side` is the only `pnp` part in the gallery, so
this one example is the entire visible symptom — but the same file's `pmos`
stamp is worth checking by whoever fixes it, since it is the other
mirrored-polarity model.

**No content change, per doctrine — engine bugs are escalated, not worked
around.** EXPECTED.md and both intros keep the correct circuit and correct
reasoning, and carry an explicit, clearly-marked note that the simulator does not
yet show it, with the predicted ~2.9 mA labelled a prediction rather than a
measurement. That is the pilot's layer-3 hazard handled in the open: an intro
must never tell a student to expect something the app will not do.

## pc33-thermistor-divider — VERDICT: content-fix

`layers: engine`, `--at 1`, control swept 0 → 1 in five steps.

**Finding — the declared parameters were inert.** `circuit.json` declared
`minOhms: 1000, maxOhms: 100000`, but the NTC model reads **`rCold` / `rHot`**
(`bw-board/src/mna.js:1259` in `stampVariableResistor`, and again at `:865` when
extracting currents). The declaration was silently discarded and the model used
its own defaults of 100 kΩ / 1 kΩ — the same two numbers, which is exactly why
nothing looked wrong. This is the pilot's finding-2 class (`R` vs `windingR`)
recurring, and the same candidate layer-1 check applies: *reject part params that
no registered device reads.*

Proof that they were inert, by declaring values that differ from the defaults:

| declaration | control 0 | control 1 |
|---|---|---|
| `minOhms: 4700, maxOhms: 22000` | 0.4545 V | 4.5455 V — unchanged, ignored |
| `rHot: 4700` (after the fix) | — | 3.4014 V = 5 · 10/14.7 ✓ |

**Action.** Renamed to `rCold: 100000, rHot: 1000`, preserving today's
behaviour while making the declaration live. Also **removed the `probe` part** —
a 1 MΩ resistor standing in for a voltmeter, which loaded the divider enough to
shift every reading off the textbook value (0.4505 V measured against the ideal
0.4545 V). With it gone the divider is exact:

| control | junction | ideal |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 0.4545 V | 5 · 10/110 |
| 0.50 | 2.5000 V | equal halves |
| 1.00 | 4.5455 V | 5 · 10/11 |

**This is the fix that was narrowed by engine-only coverage.** The engine has a
real `voltmeter` device (`devices/bench-meters.js`, 10 MΩ, would have loaded the
divider a hundred times less), and swapping the fake probe for it was the
tempting fix. But `voltmeter` appears **nowhere in `bw-circuit-ui/src`** — no
renderer, no sidecar — so on the canvas it would likely be a ghost part, and
ghosts are precisely the checklist item this audit cannot verify. Removing the
part is safe without opening the browser; adding one is not. Recorded here so
whoever does drive the app can finish the job.

## pc35-capacitor-bypass — VERDICT: content-fix (the circuit contradicted its title)

`layers: engine`, `--at 1 … 10000`.

**Finding — the capacitor was in series with the load, not across it.** The
wiring ran `led.cathode → cap.a → gnd`, so the LED and the capacitor were one
branch. EXPECTED.md's first sentence ("The capacitor is connected in parallel
with the load") described a circuit that was not on disk, and its second ("It
does not change the steady-state LED current") was the opposite of what happened:
a series capacitor blocks DC, so the LED **went out**.

| t | old (series) | new (parallel) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 ms | 2.964 mA | 0.00 mA (capacitor filling) |
| 100 ms | 2.406 mA | 0.00 mA |
| 1 s | 0.365 mA | 2.970 mA |
| 3 s | 0.006 mA | 2.970 mA |
| 10 s | **0.000 mA — dark forever** | 2.970 mA |

**Action.** Moved `cap.a` to the `r1.b`/`led.anode` node and returned
`led.cathode` to ground, which is the parallel arrangement the title, the
`program.bw` comment and EXPECTED.md all already claimed. Steady state is now the
plain 2.97 mA the LED would draw with no capacitor present — the correct result
for a bypass — and the observable is the soft turn-on as 470 µF fills through
1 kΩ. The old topology is not thrown away: it is the intro's closing experiment,
where one wire turns a bypass into a block.

**ENGINE BUG found while looking for a better demonstration — ESCALATED.**
`setPower(false)` **annihilates stored capacitor charge** instead of leaving it
to discharge into the surrounding network. Minimal repro, no example file: 5 V
source → 1 kΩ → node, node → 470 µF → ground, plus a 10 kΩ bleeder from node to
ground (τ = 4.7 s).

| t | node |
|---|---|
| 2000 ms (charged) | 4.5025 V |
| immediately after `setPower(false)` | **0.0000 V** |
| 2001 … 2100 ms | 0.0000 V |

The energy does not decay, it vanishes on the same tick. Every "cut the supply
and watch what the capacitor does" demonstration is therefore unobservable
through the app's power switch, which is a shame because that is the intuitive
way to show a reservoir. Blast radius: any discharge-on-power-loss example —
`pc43-bleeder-discharge` and `29-capacitor-charge` are the obvious candidates,
both in other slices. Consequences taken here: pc29 discharges through switches
instead, and **neither pc35 intro asks the reader to switch the power off** — the
layer-3 hazard again, caught before it shipped this time.
