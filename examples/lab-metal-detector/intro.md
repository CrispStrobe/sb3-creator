# Beat-frequency metal detector with a hand-wound coil

Two oscillators hum at almost the same pitch. Mixed together they make a third, low tone: their difference, the beat. One oscillator's coil is a large hand-wound search coil, and metal near it changes the coil and so the beat. That is a metal detector.

**Teaches:** beats and difference frequencies, inductance, eddy currents

## What to do

Click **Sim** and listen to the earphone. In the instruments column, move the **Metal** slider from 20 cm (far away) down towards 0 (a coin lying on the coil).

## What you should see

- No metal: a low hum of about 195 Hz.
- The coin getting close: the hum rises, by about 40 Hz with the coin right on the coil.
- From further than about one coil radius away, the change is small.

## Why it works

A coin is a closed ring of metal. The coil's field makes currents circle in it, and those currents push back, so the coil looks like it has less inductance. With less inductance the search oscillator runs faster, and the beat between it and the steady reference oscillator changes pitch. Iron does the opposite: it gathers the field and raises the inductance.
