# Distant stations after dark

In the evening a medium-wave radio suddenly picks up stations from hundreds of kilometres away that are silent all day. This bench lets you turn the clock and hear it happen.

**Teaches:** ground wave and sky wave, the ionosphere by day and by night

## What to do

Click **Sim**. The superhet is tuned to a distant station on 702 kHz. The **dial on the antenna** is the time of day: at noon (half-way) you hear almost nothing. Turn it to the evening, past 18:00, to about 22:00.

## What you should see

- Noon: only a faint 700 Hz tone in the loudspeaker.
- After dark: the distant station's tone comes in loud and clear.

## Why it works

By day you hear a medium-wave station only by its ground wave, which follows the earth's surface and dies away within a few hundred kilometres. Waves sent up into the sky are swallowed by the lowest layer of the ionosphere, which the sun keeps ionised. After sunset that layer fades within an hour or two; the waves then reach higher layers that reflect them back to earth far away — the sky wave. The bench gives the distant station a sky wave five hundred times stronger than its ground wave, let through only after dark.
