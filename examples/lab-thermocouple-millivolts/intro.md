# A thermocouple straight onto a millivolt meter

Twist a copper and a constantan wire together and heat the joint: a voltage of a few hundredths of a volt appears at the free ends. A sensitive meter shows it directly, without any amplifier or battery.

**Teaches:** the Seebeck effect, millivolt measurement, source and meter resistance

## What to do

Click **Sim**. Use the thermocouple's **Heat** slider or its buttons: **Room**, **Match**, **Lighter**. Watch the meter.

## What you should see

- Room temperature: the needle at zero.
- Match: about 31.5 mV.
- Lighter: about 49 mV, nearly full scale.

## Why it works

Heat pushes electrons along each wire from the hot end towards the cold end, but not equally hard in copper and in constantan. The difference appears as a voltage between the cold ends that grows with the temperature difference. The meter needs only a tenth of a milliampere for full scale, so this tiny voltage moves it on its own. Gas boilers use a thermocouple like this to check that the pilot flame is burning.
