# The magnetic field of a hand-wound coil

Wind wire around a tube, send a current through it, and the coil becomes a magnet. A compass next to it shows how strong: the more current, the further the needle turns away from north.

**Teaches:** a current makes a magnetic field, field strength and current, the Earth's field as a yardstick

## What to do

Click **Sim** and close the switch. Hover over the coil to see its current, its field and how far the compass turns. Turn the rheostat to change the current.

## What you should see

- Switch open: the compass points north.
- Full current, about 50 mA: the needle turns about 30°.
- Less current: less deflection, about 8° with the rheostat all the way in.

## Why it works

Every turn of wire carrying a current makes a little magnetic field around itself; in a coil they all add up along its axis. The compass needle lines up with the total of the coil's field and the Earth's, so it turns further the stronger the coil is compared with the Earth. Electromagnets, relays and loudspeakers all start here.
