# Faster magnet, bigger pulse

A magnet moving through a coil makes a voltage pulse in it. How big? That depends on how fast the magnet moves. Two identical coils: a magnet falls through the first from just 2 cm, through the second from 40 cm. The amplifier chip and a transistor flash an LED with the pulse.

**Teaches:** induction needs change, the induced voltage grows with speed

## What to do

Click **Sim**. With the slide switch on the left the amplifier listens to the first coil. In the instruments column press its **Drop magnet** button and watch the LED. Move the slide switch to the right and drop the magnet through the second coil.

## What you should see

- Slow magnet (first coil): a pulse of about 27 mV, the LED barely glows (about 1 mA).
- Fast magnet (second coil): about 90 mV, a bright flash (about 14 mA).
- A magnet lying still does nothing at all.

## Why it works

The coil's voltage is set by how quickly the magnetic field through it changes. A magnet falling from 40 cm passes the coil about four and a half times faster than one dropped from 2 cm, so the field changes faster and the pulse is bigger. The amplifier multiplies it by twenty; the transistor turns the amplified pulse into LED current.
