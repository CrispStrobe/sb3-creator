# A tapped coil makes a Hartley oscillator

A coil and a capacitor exchange energy back and forth at their own frequency. A transistor can keep that going if it gets back a little of the swing at the right moment. In the Hartley oscillator a tap on the coil provides exactly that.

**Teaches:** LC resonance, feedback, oscillators, tapped coil (Hartley)

## What to do

Click **Sim** and listen to the earphone. Open the oscilloscope: channel 1 is the collector end of the coil, channel 2 the other end.

## What you should see

- A high tone of about 3.8 kHz.
- The two ends of the coil move in opposite directions; the collector end swings up to almost twice the battery voltage.

## Why it works

The coil and the 100 nF capacitor form a tuned circuit. The tap sits on the battery, so when one end of the coil goes up, the other goes down. The transistor turns a rising base into a falling collector; feeding the base from the far end of the coil turns that around once more, and the loop pushes itself along at the tuned circuit's frequency.
