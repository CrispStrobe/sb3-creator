# A coil kicks a pendulum: resonance

A paper clip hangs on a thread next to an electromagnet. A two-transistor blinker sends a current pulse through the coil now and then, and each pulse pulls the clip. If the pulses come at the pendulum's own rhythm, the swing builds up. At any other rate it hardly moves.

**Teaches:** natural frequency, resonance, energy fed in step with a motion, an electromagnet's pull on iron

## What to do

Click **Sim**. The pot sets the pause between pulses; the LED lights during the pause. Start with the pot turned fully one way (fast pulses) and watch the clip swing above the coil. Then turn the pot slowly until the pulses come a little over once a second. Each time, wait a few seconds for the swing to build up.

## What you should see

- Fast pulses: the clip only twitches.
- Pulses at about 1.1 to 1.3 per second: the clip swings further each time, up to around ten degrees.
- A little slower again: the swing dies away.
- The tooltip on the pendulum shows its swing, its angle and its natural frequency (1.29 Hz for 15 cm).

## Why it works

A pendulum swings at a rate set only by its length. Each pull adds a little energy, but only if it comes while the clip is moving towards the coil. Pulses in step with the swing all push the same way, so the energy adds up until the air's damping uses up as much as each pull brings. Pulses out of step push sometimes with and sometimes against the swing, and cancel out. The coil pulls iron whichever way the current flows, and pulls hardest close to the pole. That is why the best rate is slightly below the pendulum's own.
