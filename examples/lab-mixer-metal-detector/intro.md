# Metal detector with a mixer receiver

A search oscillator runs around a hand-wound coil at about 96 kHz. A mixer chip with its own oscillator listens to it: their difference is a tone in the earphone. A coin near the search coil changes its inductance a few per cent — and the tone by thousands of hertz.

**Teaches:** beat-frequency metal detection, inductance and metal, mixing two oscillators

## What to do

Click **Sim** and listen. Then move the **Metal** slider at the search coil to 0 cm (a coin on the coil). Finally turn the tuning knob to 0.3.

## What you should see

- A tone of about 500 Hz without metal.
- With the coin: a much higher tone, about 3 kHz.
- At 0.3 the two oscillators almost meet: the tone disappears (zero beat).

## Why it works

The mixer multiplies the search oscillator by its own: the difference frequency is what you hear. The coin lets eddy currents flow that weaken the coil's magnetic field, so its inductance falls and the search oscillator speeds up. A shift of 2.5 kHz out of 96 kHz is small for the oscillator but huge for the difference tone. Set the receiver to zero beat and even a tiny shift becomes audible.
