# A battery on the tongue: electrolysis you can taste

Touch a 9 V battery to your tongue and it tingles and tastes sour. A 1.5 V cell hardly does anything. The reason is chemistry: current can only get from the metal into the wet tongue by splitting water, and that needs a minimum voltage.

**Teaches:** electrolytes, decomposition voltage, electrode polarisation

## What to do

Click **Sim**. Hold the push button (that is the tongue touching the contacts) and read the ammeter. Hover over the tongue to see the electrolysis current and the taste. Let go and watch the voltmeter. Then slide the switch to the left (1.5 V) and touch again.

## What you should see

- 9 V: about 3.5 mA, and a strong taste.
- 1.5 V: less than a microamp, no taste.
- After letting go: almost 2 V still on the contacts, slowly fading.

## Why it works

In a wire, electrons simply flow. In the wet tongue there are no free electrons, only ions, so at each contact a chemical reaction has to hand the charge over: water is split, and acid forms at the + contact. That reaction needs about 1.7 V before it gets going, which a 1.5 V cell does not have. The products left on the contacts make them a little battery for a moment.
