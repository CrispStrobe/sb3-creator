# Beats between a transistor oscillator and the PLL oscillator

Two tones that are almost the same pitch, played together, do not sound like two tones: you hear one note that swells and fades, a "wah-wah". Here one tone comes from a transistor oscillator, the other from a PLL chip's oscillator, and an amplifier mixes them.

**Teaches:** superposition, beat frequency, tuning by ear

## What to do

Click **Sim** and listen. Turn the tune potentiometer slowly and listen to the swelling get faster or slower.

## What you should see

- The sound pulses about 80 times a second as built.
- The closer the two pitches, the slower the pulsing; when they match it stops.

## Why it works

The two oscillators run at slightly different speeds, so one keeps gaining on the other. When their waves are in step they reinforce (or here, cancel in the amplifier's difference), half a beat later they are opposite and do the reverse. The pulsing rate is the difference of the two frequencies. Musicians tune their instruments by making these beats slow down until they vanish.
