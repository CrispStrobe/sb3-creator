# A spark transmitter

The first radio transmitters had no oscillator at all: a spark switched a current on and off, and receivers heard a click wherever they were tuned. Here a push button dumps a charged capacitor through a coil, and a radio a short distance away clicks.

**Teaches:** a current step is broadband, impulse radiation, why sparks interfere with radio

## What to do

Click **Sim**. Turn the radio's tuning knob to 0.15 (between stations) and press the button. Then tune to 0.5 and press again.

## What you should see

- Between stations: silence, and a loud click at every press.
- On the 612 kHz station: its tone, and the click on top of it.

## Why it works

The button's contact bounces, so the coil's current switches on and off several times in a few milliseconds, each time in an instant. The coil's magnetic field changes just as suddenly and reaches the receiving rod. A sudden change contains every frequency, so the receiver's tuned circuit is struck like a bell at whatever frequency it is set to, and the detector turns the ringing into a click.
