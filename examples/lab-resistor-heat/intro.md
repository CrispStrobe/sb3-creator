# A resistor turns into a heater

Any resistor that carries current turns electrical power into heat: P = V²/R. A small resistance straight across a battery takes a large current and becomes noticeably warm, like the element of a heater. A diode glued to each resistor works as a thermometer: its forward voltage drops about 2 mV for every degree it warms.

**Teaches:** electrical power P = V²/R, power turning into heat, a diode junction as a thermometer

## What to do

Click **Sim** and watch voltmeter 1 for a few seconds: the 100 Ω resistor is connected. Then flip the slide switch to the 1 kΩ resistor and watch both voltmeters.

## What you should see

- Voltmeter 1 falls from 0.580 V by about 50 mV in a few seconds: its resistor is about 30 °C warmer than the room.
- After switching, voltmeter 1 climbs back as that resistor cools; voltmeter 2 hardly moves, because the 1 kΩ resistor takes only a tenth of the power.

## Why it works

With 100 Ω across 9 V, 90 mA flow and 0.81 W turn into heat; the resistor warms until it gives that heat to the air as fast as it gets it. Ten times the resistance means a tenth of the current at the same voltage, so a tenth of the power. The diodes see their resistor's warmth, and a warmer silicon junction needs less voltage to pass the same current.
