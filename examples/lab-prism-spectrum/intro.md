# Prism and slit: colours of LEDs and a laser

A prism bends blue light more than red, so it fans white light out into its colours. A slit in front of a light sensor picks one direction at a time. Sweep the slit and you read the spectrum of the LED.

**Teaches:** dispersion, spectra of LEDs, the single wavelength of a laser

## What to do

Click **Sim** and turn the knob slowly across its range, noting where the meter rises. In the inspector change the LED's colour to red or blue and sweep again. Finally replace the LED by a laser diode.

## What you should see

- White LED: a strong peak at about 36.6° (yellow-green) and a smaller one at about 37.2° (blue).
- Red LED: one peak at about 36.3°.
- Laser: one very narrow line.

## Why it works

Glass and plastic slow blue light a little more than red light, so at each surface of the prism blue is bent more. A white LED is really a blue LED with a yellow glowing coating, so its light falls apart into those two parts. A laser produces a single wavelength, so it leaves the prism as one thin line.
