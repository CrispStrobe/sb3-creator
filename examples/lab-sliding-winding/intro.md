# Tuning with the coil

A tuned circuit has two knobs, not one: its capacitance and its inductance. Here the inductance changes in two ways: by sliding a small winding along the ferrite rod, and by connecting the two windings in series one way round or the other.

**Teaches:** inductance of a coil on a ferrite rod, windings in series aiding and opposing, f = 1/(2π√LC)

## What to do

Click **Sim**. Both slide switches left (windings aiding), the rod's dial at 0, the tuning knob at 0.5: silence. Now flip both slide switches right. Then flip them back, turn the rod's dial to the end (100 %) and the tuning knob to 0.45.

## What you should see

- Aiding, dial 0.5: no station.
- Opposing: the 810 kHz station with its two tones.
- Aiding, winding slid to the end, dial 0.45: the 612 kHz station.

## Why it works

In series, two windings on one rod add up to more than their sum when their fields point the same way (each induces extra voltage in the other) and to less when they work against each other. More inductance tunes lower, less tunes higher. A winding slid off the middle of the rod has less ferrite inside it, so its inductance drops and the station has to be found again with the tuning knob.
